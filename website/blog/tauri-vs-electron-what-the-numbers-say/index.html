Tauri vs. Electron: What the Numbers Say, and What They Don't

2026-10-11 · sabaoongfx · 3 min read

Almost every comparison of Tauri and Electron opens with a table showing Tauri winning by a wide margin. The direction is right. The exact numbers are much less reliable than they look, and it is worth knowing why before you quote them.

The basic difference

Electron bundles a full copy of Chromium and Node.js with every app. That gives you an identical rendering engine on every operating system, but each app carries its own browser. Tauri takes the opposite approach: the app uses the web view that already ships with the operating system (WebView2 on Windows, WebKit on macOS and Linux), and the backend is written in Rust. Less is bundled, so there is less to download and, usually, less to keep in memory.

What published comparisons report

A survey of 2026 articles shows a consistent direction and inconsistent figures:

  • Installed size. One source reports a hello-world Tauri build near 3 MB against about 96 MB for Electron on Windows. Another gives Tauri apps 2 to 10 MB against 80 to 200 MB for Electron.
  • Idle memory. One comparison puts a single window at around 80 MB for Tauri versus around 160 MB for Electron. Another claims 30 to 50 MB for Tauri. A third says Tauri uses roughly half as much, without naming its benchmark.
  • Startup time. One report gives 380 ms for Tauri against 1,420 ms for Electron; another gives about 190 ms against about 640 ms on an M2 MacBook Air.

Those memory figures differ by a factor of two or more from each other. That is not a contradiction so much as a sign that these are different apps, different operating systems, different Electron versions (the articles cite everything from 33 to 40) and different ways of counting memory. Some of the sources are also vendor-adjacent blogs rather than independent benchmarks.

Why memory is hard to compare

Electron runs several processes: a main process, one or more renderers, and helpers. Tauri’s web view is also multi-process on some platforms, and part of its memory is owned by the operating system, not by your app. So “how much does it use” depends on whether you count shared pages, whether you add up every child process, and which tool you used. We explain the counting problem in memory explained. The fair method is to sum all of an app’s processes in a single tool and compare like with like.

What the numbers leave out

Tauri’s advantages come with real costs:

  • Rendering differences. Because each platform uses a different web view, a layout or feature can behave differently on Windows, macOS and Linux. Electron gives you one engine everywhere.
  • Ecosystem maturity. Electron has years more tooling, documentation and answered questions.
  • A second language. Backend work means Rust. That is a strength for performance and safety and a barrier for teams that only know JavaScript.

Measure it yourself

If the choice matters for your project, build the same small screen both ways and compare. Launch each, wait thirty seconds for it to settle, then open a task manager, group by application and read the combined memory of every process belonging to the app. Do it on each operating system you support. Open Task Manager’s Processes tab groups child processes under one row for exactly this kind of check.

We chose Tauri for this project because a tool that reports what is using your memory should not itself be a heavy item on the list. We haven’t published a head-to-head benchmark of our own, and we would rather say so than borrow someone else’s numbers.
